What actually happens to your accounts when you die

Open research on digital legacy. Every claim here links to the vendor's own documentation, and everything we could not verify is listed in the open rather than quietly dropped.

There are exactly two hard questions in this category. Does the provider actually verify that you died — or does it just measure whether you stopped logging in? And can the provider read your data? Almost every product answers one of them well. We have not found one that answers both.

The teardown

Every digital-legacy service, password manager and platform legacy-contact feature, checked against its own live documentation. Includes the retractions we made to our own earlier drafts.

Answers to the questions people actually ask

Forty-seven questions about inheritance, passwords, photos, money and account access — answered properly, once, with sources. Each one on its own page, because each one is a different question.

The comparison, as an open dataset

All sixteen providers as JSON and CSV, under CC BY 4.0 — every field carrying the source URL it was read from, and every field we could not establish marked unknown rather than guessed. Recompute it, extend it, or disagree with it precisely.

How to read anything here

We built a product in this category, so we are not a neutral party. That is precisely why every claim is a link to someone else's words rather than to our characterisation of them. Check them.

If you are a vendor named in this research and think we have described your product wrongly, tell us and we will correct it in the text and record the correction publicly.

Corrections are welcome as an issue on the relevant repository, or through the contact form at cairnvault.app/contact.
